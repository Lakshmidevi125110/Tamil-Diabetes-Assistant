# Diabetes overview (WHO)

## What diabetes is
Diabetes is a long-term (chronic) condition. It happens when the pancreas does not make enough insulin, or when the body cannot use the insulin it makes effectively. Insulin is a hormone that helps regulate blood glucose (blood sugar). When blood glucose stays high for a long time (hyperglycaemia), it can gradually damage the body, especially the nerves and blood vessels.

## Types of diabetes
- **Type 1 diabetes:** The body produces very little or no insulin, so daily insulin is needed. WHO says the cause and a way to prevent it are not known.
- **Type 2 diabetes:** The body cannot use insulin properly. It makes up more than 95% of all diabetes cases and is often preventable.
- **Gestational diabetes:** Blood glucose is higher than normal but below the level used to diagnose diabetes, and it appears during pregnancy. It is usually found through screening during prenatal care rather than through symptoms.
- **Impaired glucose tolerance and impaired fasting glycaemia:** These are in-between states. People with them have a high chance of developing type 2 diabetes, but it is not certain that they will.

## Common symptoms
- Feeling very thirsty
- Passing urine more often than usual
- Blurred vision
- Feeling tired
- Losing weight without trying

Symptoms of type 2 diabetes can be mild and may take years to be noticed.

## Risk factors for type 2 diabetes
The page mentions being overweight, not getting enough physical activity, and genetics.

## Scale of the problem (WHO figures)
- About 830 million people were living with diabetes in 2022, compared with 200 million in 1990.
- In 2022, 59% of adults aged 30 and over with diabetes were not taking medicine for it.
- Diabetes directly caused about 1.6 million deaths in 2021.

This is general educational information summarised from the WHO page. It does not diagnose any condition. A qualified healthcare professional can interpret symptoms and test results for an individual.
