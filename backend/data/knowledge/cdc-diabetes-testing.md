# Diabetes testing (CDC)

These values come from the CDC page and are written for the United States. They are used by doctors to interpret test results. Guidelines in other countries can differ, so a person should ask their own doctor how results apply to them.

## Types of tests
- **A1C test:** Shows average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months. Normal is below 5.7%, prediabetes is 5.7% to 6.4%, and diabetes is 6.5% or above.
- **Fasting blood sugar test:** Done after an overnight fast. Normal is 99 mg/dL or below, prediabetes is 100 to 125 mg/dL, and diabetes is 126 mg/dL or above.
- **Glucose tolerance test:** Blood sugar is measured while fasting and again after drinking a sugary liquid, usually at 1, 2 and sometimes 3 hours. At 2 hours, normal is 140 mg/dL or below, prediabetes is 140 to 199 mg/dL, and diabetes is 200 mg/dL or above.
- **Random blood sugar test:** Done at any time without fasting. A result of 200 mg/dL or above points to diabetes.
- **Autoantibody test:** A blood test that looks for substances linked to type 1 diabetes.
- **Urine ketone test:** Mentioned in relation to type 1 diabetes.
- **Gestational diabetes tests:** A glucose screening test (a sugary drink followed by a blood sample 1 hour later, where 140 mg/dL or lower is normal), and a glucose tolerance test if the screening result is higher.

## Who should talk to a doctor about testing
- People with diabetes risk factors can ask their doctor about screening, even without symptoms.
- Doctors may test for type 1 diabetes when it is suspected or when someone is at high risk.
- Gestational diabetes is usually tested between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy, and earlier for people at higher risk.

## After a test at a health fair or pharmacy
The page advises following up with a doctor, who may repeat the test to confirm the result.

A single test result is not a diagnosis. Diagnosis is made by a healthcare professional. This is general educational information summarised from the CDC page.
