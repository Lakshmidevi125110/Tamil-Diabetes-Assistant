# Diabetes complications, prevention and care (WHO)

## Possible complications over time
When blood glucose stays high for years, the page describes risks to several parts of the body:
- **Heart and blood vessels:** higher risk of heart attack and stroke.
- **Kidneys:** kidney failure.
- **Eyes:** damage to the small blood vessels in the eye can lead to permanent vision loss.
- **Feet and nerves:** nerve damage and poor blood flow, which together raise the chance of foot ulcers, infection and, in serious cases, amputation.
- **Pregnancy:** women with gestational diabetes face higher risks during pregnancy and delivery. They, and possibly their children, also have a higher chance of type 2 diabetes later in life.

## Early detection
Diabetes can be found with relatively inexpensive blood glucose tests. WHO says regular check-ups and blood tests with a healthcare provider are the best way to detect it early.

## Prevention (mainly type 2 diabetes)
The page lists these steps:
- Reach and keep a healthy body weight.
- Be physically active, with at least 150 minutes of moderate activity each week.
- Eat a healthy diet and avoid sugar and saturated fat.
- Avoid tobacco.

## Care and treatment overview
- A healthy lifestyle is described as one of the most important parts of care.
- Type 1 diabetes requires insulin.
- Some people with type 2 diabetes also need medicines, which may include insulin. The choice of medicine is made by their healthcare team.
- People with diabetes may also need care to protect the blood vessels, kidneys, eyes and feet. This can include blood pressure management, foot care, kidney screening and regular eye examinations.

This is general educational information summarised from the WHO page. It is not a treatment plan, and medicines should only be started, changed or stopped by a qualified healthcare professional.
