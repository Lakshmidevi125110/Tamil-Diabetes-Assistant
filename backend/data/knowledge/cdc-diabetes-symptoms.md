# Symptoms of diabetes (CDC)

## Symptoms that can occur in both type 1 and type 2
- Passing urine more often
- Feeling more thirsty and hungry than usual
- Losing weight without trying
- Feeling tired
- Irritability or mood changes
- Blurred vision
- Urinary tract infections or yeast infections that keep coming back

## Additional symptoms mentioned for type 1
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Stomach pain
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (a serious complication)
- In children, unexplained bed-wetting or more accidents than usual

## Additional symptoms mentioned for type 2
- Cuts, sores or wounds that heal very slowly
- Dark patches of skin around the neck, armpits or groin
- Numbness or tingling in the hands or feet

## Diabetes can exist without symptoms
- Early type 1 diabetes may have no symptoms.
- Type 2 diabetes symptoms can take several years to develop, and some people never notice any.
- Gestational diabetes usually has no symptoms.

## Gestational diabetes testing
The page says a doctor should test for gestational diabetes between 24 and 28 weeks of pregnancy. If lifestyle changes do not keep blood sugar under control, a doctor may prescribe medicine.

These symptoms can have many causes, and having one does not mean a person has diabetes. A healthcare professional can arrange the right tests. This is general educational information summarised from the CDC page.
