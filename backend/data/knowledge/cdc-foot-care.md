# Foot care and diabetes (CDC)

## Why foot care matters
About half of people with diabetes have nerve damage, most often in the feet and legs. Nerve damage can reduce feeling, so a person may not notice a cut, blister or sore. Foot ulcers that are not treated can become infected and heal poorly, and serious infections may lead to amputation. Keeping blood sugar within the target range is one of the most important ways to prevent or slow nerve damage.

## Everyday foot care steps listed on the page
- Check both feet every day for cuts, redness, swelling, sores, blisters, corns, calluses, and changes to skin or nails. A mirror or another person can help if it is hard to see.
- Wash feet daily in warm water, dry them well, and moisturise the tops and bottoms, but not between the toes.
- Wear shoes, socks or slippers at all times, even indoors, and check inside shoes for objects before putting them on.
- Choose well-fitting shoes, ideally bought when feet are largest (end of the day), and break new shoes in gradually.
- Trim toenails straight across and smooth sharp edges with a file. A foot specialist (podiatrist) can help if nails are hard to reach.
- Do not remove corns or calluses yourself, and avoid over-the-counter removal products.
- Help blood flow by raising feet when sitting and wiggling the toes several times a day.
- Ask a doctor which activities are suitable.
- Have feet checked at every primary care visit, and see a foot specialist once a year for a full exam, or more often if there is nerve damage.

## Warning signs to tell a doctor about
- Leg pain or cramping in the buttocks, thighs or calves during activity
- Tingling, burning or pain in the feet
- Less ability to feel touch, heat or cold
- Changes in the shape of the foot over time
- Hair loss on the toes, feet or lower legs
- Dry, cracked skin
- Changes in foot colour or temperature
- Thick, yellow toenails
- Fungal infections such as athlete's foot
- A blister, sore, ulcer, infected corn or ingrown toenail

The page advises contacting a doctor or foot specialist right away if any warning sign appears, rather than waiting for the next appointment.
