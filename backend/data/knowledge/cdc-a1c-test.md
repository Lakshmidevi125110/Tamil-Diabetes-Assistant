# The A1C (HbA1c) test (CDC)

## What it measures
The A1C test, also called the hemoglobin A1C or HbA1c test, is a simple blood test. It shows a person's average blood sugar over roughly the past 3 months. This is different from a home blood sugar check, which shows the level at one moment. The 3-month window exists because red blood cells renew about every 3 months. The test measures the percentage of red blood cells that have sugar attached to hemoglobin.

## How it is used
- To help diagnose prediabetes and diabetes.
- To monitor how well diabetes is being managed over time.
- It does not replace checking blood sugar at home.
- No fasting is needed for this test.
- Testing is recommended for people over 45, and for younger people who are overweight or have another risk factor for prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

## A1C ranges given on the page (US guidance)
- Normal: below 5.7%
- Prediabetes: 5.7% to 6.4%
- Diabetes: 6.5% or above

## How often it is repeated (as described on the page)
- Normal result: the doctor sets a re-testing schedule based on age and risk factors.
- Prediabetes: repeated about every 1 to 2 years.
- Diabetes: most people are tested at least twice a year, sometimes more often depending on their plan or other health conditions.

## Things that can affect the result
The A1C average may not show short spikes or dips in blood sugar. Some conditions can make the result read falsely high or low, including severe anemia, kidney failure, liver disease, some blood disorders (such as sickle cell disease or thalassemia), some medicines, blood loss or transfusion, and early or late pregnancy. The page advises telling the doctor if any of these apply.

A single result is not a diagnosis, and individual targets are set by a healthcare professional. This is general educational information summarised from the CDC page.
