# Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and when to seek urgent care (CDC)

## What DKA is
Diabetic ketoacidosis is a serious, potentially life-threatening complication of diabetes. It is most common in people with type 1 diabetes, though people with type 2 diabetes can develop it too. It happens when there is too little insulin for sugar to enter the cells for energy. The liver then breaks down fat for fuel, which makes acids called ketones, and these can build up to dangerous levels. Sometimes DKA is the first sign of diabetes in someone who has not yet been diagnosed.

## Warning signs
- **Early signs, which usually develop slowly:** intense thirst and passing urine very often.
- **More serious signs, which can appear quickly:** fast, deep breathing; dry skin and mouth; a flushed face; fruity-smelling breath; headache; muscle stiffness or aches; extreme tiredness; nausea and vomiting; stomach pain.

## Common causes
- Illness, which can make eating and drinking difficult and blood sugar harder to manage
- Missed insulin doses, a blocked insulin pump, or an incorrect insulin dose
- Less often: heart attack, stroke, physical injury, alcohol or drug use, and certain medicines

## When the page says to get emergency care
The page says to go to the emergency room or call emergency services right away (the page is written for the United States, where the number is 911) if a person has any of the following:
- Blood sugar that stays at 300 mg/dL or above
- Fruity-smelling breath
- Vomiting and being unable to keep food or drinks down
- Trouble breathing
- Several signs and symptoms of DKA together

The page also says that elevated ketones are a sign of DKA, which is a medical emergency needing immediate treatment.

## Checking when unwell
For a person with diabetes who is sick, or whose blood sugar is 250 mg/dL or above, the page advises checking blood sugar every 4 to 6 hours and checking urine for ketones.

This is general educational information summarised from the CDC page. It is not a substitute for urgent medical care. Anyone with severe symptoms should seek emergency help immediately.
