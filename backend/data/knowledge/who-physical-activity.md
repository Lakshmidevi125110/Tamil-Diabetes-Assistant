# Physical activity (WHO)

## General recommendation for adults
WHO recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week for adults. The page says the WHO guidelines also cover adults living with chronic conditions, but it does not give separate figures for them on this page.

## Benefits for adults described by WHO
- Lower risk of death from any cause and from cardiovascular disease
- Lower risk of high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, some cancers and falls
- Better mental health, thinking skills (cognitive health) and sleep
- Better measures of body fat
- Helps prevent and manage noncommunicable diseases, and can reduce symptoms of depression and anxiety

## Sitting for long periods
Sedentary behaviour means low-energy activity while awake, such as sitting, reclining or lying down. WHO says more sedentary time is linked to higher risks of death, cardiovascular disease, cancer and type 2 diabetes, and that people of all ages should limit the time they spend sitting.

This is a general population recommendation. People with diabetes or other conditions should ask their healthcare professional which activities suit them. This is general educational information summarised from the WHO page.
