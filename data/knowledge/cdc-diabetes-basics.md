# Diabetes basics (CDC)

## What diabetes is
Diabetes is a long-lasting condition that affects how the body turns food into energy. Food is broken down into sugar in the blood. In response, the body releases insulin, which moves that sugar into cells to be used for energy. In diabetes, the body either makes too little insulin or cannot use it well, so too much sugar stays in the blood.

Over time, high blood sugar can lead to problems such as heart disease, vision loss and kidney disease. There is currently no cure for diabetes, but weight loss, healthy eating and regular physical activity can help.

## Types of diabetes
- **Type 1:** Thought to be caused by an autoimmune reaction in which the body stops making insulin. People with type 1 need insulin every day. It is usually diagnosed in children and young adults but can happen at any age. There is no known way to prevent it.
- **Type 2:** The body does not use insulin well and cannot keep blood sugar at a normal level. It develops over many years and is usually diagnosed in adults, although it is increasingly seen in children, teenagers and young adults. It can be prevented or delayed with lifestyle changes.
- **Gestational:** Develops during pregnancy in women who did not have diabetes before. It usually goes away after the baby is born, but it raises the mother's chance of type 2 diabetes later. The baby also has a higher chance of obesity and type 2 diabetes later in life.

## Symptoms and awareness
Early type 1 diabetes may cause no symptoms, and symptoms may then appear suddenly and strongly over weeks or months. Type 2 diabetes may also cause no noticeable symptoms for a long time, which is why blood sugar testing is advised for people at risk.

## Additional points from the page (United States data)
- Type 2 makes up about 90% to 95% of diagnosed cases and type 1 about 5% to 10%.
- Diabetes is a leading cause of kidney failure, lower-limb amputation and adult blindness.
