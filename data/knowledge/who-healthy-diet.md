# Healthy diet for adults (WHO)

The WHO date shown on the page is 26 January 2026. The page does not say whether it is the first publication date or the last update.

## What WHO recommends for adults
- **Fruits and vegetables:** at least 400 grams a day.
- **Free sugars:** less than 10% of total daily energy. For a person of healthy weight eating about 2000 calories a day, that is about 50 grams (roughly 12 level teaspoons). Going down to 5% or less may bring extra benefits.
- **Fat:** total fat of 30% or less of daily energy; saturated fat under 10%; trans fat under 1%. Fat should still provide at least 15% of daily energy.
- **Salt:** less than 5 grams of salt a day (equal to 2 grams of sodium).
- **Potassium:** at least 3510 mg a day, which can help offset the effect of sodium on blood pressure.
- **Fibre and whole grains:** at least 25 grams of naturally occurring fibre a day. Carbohydrates should come mainly from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses, and make up roughly 45% to 75% of daily energy.

## Benefits WHO describes
Healthy diets help protect against malnutrition in all its forms and against noncommunicable diseases including diabetes, heart disease, stroke and cancer. Less sodium supports lower blood pressure.

This is a general population recommendation and not an individual diet plan for a person with diabetes. A registered dietitian or doctor can advise on personal needs. This is general educational information summarised from the WHO page.
