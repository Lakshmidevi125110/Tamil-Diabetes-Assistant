# Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) (CDC)

## What it is
Blood sugar below 70 mg/dL is considered low (hypoglycemia). The page says it can be dangerous if left untreated and is especially common in people with type 1 diabetes. Blood sugar below 54 mg/dL is described as severe low blood sugar.

## Symptoms
- **Common:** fast heartbeat, shaking, sweating, nervousness or anxiety, irritability or confusion, dizziness and hunger.
- **Severe:** weakness, trouble walking or seeing clearly, strange behaviour or confusion, and seizures. Blood sugar this low can cause fainting. A person with severe low blood sugar will need someone else to help them.
- **Hypoglycemia unawareness:** some people have no warning symptoms. This is more likely if someone has had diabetes for more than 5 to 10 years, often has lows, or takes certain medicines such as beta blockers.

## Things that can contribute
- Too much insulin, or insulin taken at the wrong time
- Not eating enough carbohydrate for the insulin dose
- Physical activity, including how much and when
- Drinking alcohol
- The fat, protein and fibre content of meals
- Hot and humid weather, or high altitude
- Unexpected changes in daily routine
- Puberty and menstruation

## Ideas the page gives for reducing the chance of lows
- Eat regular meals and do not skip them. This can help avoid lows at night.
- People at risk of lows overnight may be advised to have a snack before bed, and to eat when drinking alcohol.
- A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) can alert a person while they sleep.
- People with hypoglycemia unawareness may need to check blood sugar more often, especially before driving or physical activity.

## Notes
The page does not give emergency instructions of its own. It mentions confusion, seizures and fainting as severe signs, and refers readers to a separate CDC page on treating low blood sugar. Anyone with severe symptoms, or who is unable to look after themselves, needs urgent medical help. Questions about medicines, insulin or what to do for lows should go to a healthcare professional.
